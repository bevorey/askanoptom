Dry Eye

Dry eye is one of the most common conditions optometrists treat. Here's what you need to know and how to manage it at home.

💧

How to apply eye drops correctly

  1. Wash your hands thoroughly before touching your eyes.
  2. Tilt your head back and look up at the ceiling.
  3. Gently pull down your lower eyelid to create a small pocket.
  4. Hold the bottle above the eye — do not let the tip touch your eye or lashes.
  5. Squeeze one drop into the pocket of your lower lid.
  6. Close your eye gently for 1–2 minutes. Do not blink rapidly.
  7. Press lightly on the inner corner of your eye (the tear duct) to prevent drainage.
  8. If using more than one drop, wait at least 5 minutes between drops.

Warm compresses for dry eye

  1. Soak a clean cloth in warm (not hot) water.
  2. Wring it out and place it over your closed eyelids.
  3. Hold for 10 minutes — reheat as needed to maintain warmth.
  4. After removing, gently massage your closed eyelids in a circular motion for 30 seconds.
  5. Follow immediately with a lid scrub if recommended by your optometrist.
  6. Repeat once or twice daily for best results.

⚠️

When to seek care

  • Sudden change in vision alongside dryness
  • Severe pain or significant redness
  • Sensitivity to light (photophobia)
  • No improvement after 2 weeks of consistent drops
  • Discharge or crusting around the eyes

Contact Lenses

Contact lenses are medical devices. Proper care is essential for keeping your eyes healthy.

✅

Do's

  • Always wash and dry your hands before handling lenses.
  • Follow the replacement schedule your optometrist prescribed — daily, bi-weekly, or monthly.
  • Use only contact lens solution recommended by your optometrist.
  • Clean your lens case daily and replace it every 3 months.
  • Remove lenses before swimming, showering, or using a hot tub.
  • Store lenses in fresh solution — never top up old solution.
  • Remove lenses if your eyes become red, irritated, or your vision changes.
  • Attend all scheduled contact lens check-ups.
❌

Don'ts

  • Never sleep in lenses unless specifically prescribed for overnight wear.
  • Never rinse lenses or cases with tap water — it contains microorganisms that can cause serious infection.
  • Never wear lenses past their expiry date.
  • Never share lenses with anyone else.
  • Never use saliva to wet your lenses.
  • Never ignore pain, redness, or blurred vision while wearing lenses — remove them immediately.
  • Never wear cosmetic lenses bought without a prescription.
⚠️

Warning signs — remove immediately

  • Sudden blurred vision that doesn't clear with blinking
  • Eye pain or significant discomfort
  • Unusual redness or discharge
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Feeling that something is in your eye

If symptoms persist after removal, seek same-day care from your optometrist or emergency eye service.

Nutrition & Eye Health

What you eat directly affects your eye health. Here's what the evidence says about nutrition and vision.

🥬

Foods that protect your eyes

  • Dark leafy greens (spinach, kale, collard greens) — rich in lutein and zeaxanthin, which protect the macula from oxidative damage.
  • Orange and yellow vegetables (carrots, sweet potato, butternut squash) — high in beta-carotene, converted to vitamin A which supports night vision.
  • Oily fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) — omega-3 fatty acids support tear film quality and reduce dry eye risk.
  • Eggs — contain lutein, zeaxanthin, and zinc, all beneficial for macular health.
  • Citrus fruits and berries — vitamin C supports the health of blood vessels in the eye and reduces cataract risk.
  • Nuts and seeds — vitamin E protects eye cells from free radical damage.

Supplements for AMD (AREDS2 formula)

If you have been diagnosed with age-related macular degeneration (AMD), your optometrist may recommend the AREDS2 supplement formula. This was developed from large clinical trials and contains:

  • Vitamin C — 500mg
  • Vitamin E — 400 IU
  • Lutein — 10mg
  • Zeaxanthin — 2mg
  • Zinc — 80mg
  • Copper — 2mg

Only take AREDS2 supplements if recommended by your optometrist or ophthalmologist. They are not a general eye vitamin and are specifically for people with intermediate or advanced AMD.

General eye health supplements

  • Omega-3 (fish oil or algae-based) — 1000mg daily supports tear production and reduces dry eye symptoms.
  • Lutein & Zeaxanthin — 10mg / 2mg daily supports macular pigment density.
  • Vitamin D — deficiency has been linked to dry eye and retinal changes.
  • Zinc — supports the transport of vitamin A from the liver to the retina.

Always consult your optometrist or GP before starting any supplement regimen.

Screen Time & Myopia

Screen time is reshaping how the world sees — literally. Here's what the evidence says and what you can do about it.

📱

The 20-20-20 rule

Every 20 minutes of screen time, look at something 20 feet (6 metres) away for at least 20 seconds. This relaxes the focusing muscles inside your eye and reduces digital eye strain.

  • Set a timer or use an app to remind you every 20 minutes.
  • Look out a window at a distant object — a tree, a building, the horizon.
  • Blink fully and deliberately during your break — screen use reduces blink rate by up to 60%.
  • Stand up and take a short walk if possible — your posture affects your eyes too.

🌳

Outdoor time and myopia in children

This is one of the most important things parents can do. Research consistently shows that children who spend more time outdoors have a significantly lower risk of developing myopia — and slower progression if they already have it.

  • Target 90 minutes of outdoor time daily for children — not necessarily sport, just being outside in natural light.
  • Natural light exposure triggers dopamine release in the retina, which helps regulate eye growth.
  • Morning and afternoon outdoor play is particularly beneficial — bright daylight, not just overcast skies.
  • Reducing near work alone is not enough — the outdoor light exposure is the key factor.
  • Screen time and near work increase myopia risk, but outdoor time can counteract this.

💡

Screen habits that protect your eyes

  • Screen brightness — match it to your ambient lighting. A screen that glows brightly in a dark room strains your eyes.
  • Screen distance — keep screens at arm's length (50–70cm). Phones are often held too close.
  • Night mode / warm light — switching to warmer screen tones in the evening reduces blue light exposure and improves sleep quality.
  • No screens 1 hour before bed — blue light suppresses melatonin and disrupts sleep, which in turn affects eye health.
  • Posture — screens should be slightly below eye level. Looking up at a screen increases dry eye risk by widening the eye opening.

Lid Hygiene & Procedures

Healthy eyelids are the foundation of healthy eyes. These are the techniques your optometrist may recommend at home.

🧴

How to perform a lid scrub

Lid scrubs remove debris, bacteria, and blocked oil from the eyelid margins. Your optometrist may recommend these if you have blepharitis, meibomian gland dysfunction, or recurrent styes.

  1. Wash your hands thoroughly.
  2. Apply a small amount of baby shampoo or specialist lid cleanser (e.g. Blephaclean, Cliradex) to a cotton pad, cotton bud, or clean fingertip.
  3. Close your eye and gently scrub along the base of your lashes — upper and lower lid — using small horizontal strokes.
  4. Rinse with clean water or a sterile saline solution.
  5. Repeat on the other eye using a fresh pad or bud.
  6. Follow with warm compresses if instructed by your optometrist.

🔄

Lid massage for meibomian gland dysfunction

Meibomian glands in your eyelids produce the oily layer of your tear film. When they become blocked, tears evaporate quickly causing dry eye. Lid massage helps unblock them.

  1. First, apply a warm compress for 10 minutes to soften the meibomian secretions.
  2. With a clean finger, place gentle pressure on the outer edge of the eyelid (above the lashes on the upper lid, below on the lower lid).
  3. Sweep your finger toward the lash line using firm but gentle pressure — as if squeezing a tube of toothpaste toward the opening.
  4. Repeat 10 times on each lid.
  5. You may see a small amount of clear or slightly cloudy oil expressed — this is normal.
  6. Follow with a lid scrub to clean away expressed secretions.

📅

Recommended daily routine

For patients with blepharitis, dry eye, or MGD — consistency is everything. Here's a suggested routine:

  • Morning: Warm compress (5–10 min) → Lid massage → Lid scrub → Lubricating drops
  • During the day: Lubricating drops as needed. Follow 20-20-20 rule if using screens.
  • Evening: Warm compress (5–10 min) → Lid scrub → Lubricating gel or ointment (if prescribed)

Results take 4–6 weeks of consistent treatment. Don't stop if you don't see immediate improvement.
