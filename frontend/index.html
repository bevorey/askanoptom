What is a refraction?

A refraction is the test where your optometrist determines your glasses or contact lens prescription — the "which is better, 1 or 2?" part of your eye exam. It measures how light bends as it enters your eye to find your optimal correction.

What is a PD (pupillary distance)?

Your PD is the distance in millimetres between the centres of your pupils. It's essential for making glasses — it tells the lab exactly where to position the optical centre of each lens so your vision is clear and comfortable.

What is a progressive lens?

A progressive (or multifocal) lens has no visible line and provides correction for distance, intermediate, and near vision all in one lens. They're typically prescribed for people over 40 who need correction at multiple distances.

What is a single vision lens?

A single vision lens has one focal power throughout the entire lens — correcting either distance or near vision, but not both. They're the most common type of lens and suit most patients under 40.

What causes dry eyes?

Dry eye occurs when your eyes don't produce enough tears or the tears evaporate too quickly. Common causes include screen time, air conditioning, contact lens wear, certain medications, and age-related changes in tear production.

Are my headaches from my eyes?

Eye strain, uncorrected refractive errors, and binocular vision problems can all cause headaches — particularly around the temples, forehead, or behind the eyes after prolonged screen or reading time. An optometrist can help determine if your eyes are the cause.
